---
title: "The Lamp at the End of the Pier"
author: "A. Sample"
running_title: "The Lamp"
---

Nobody on the island could say who lit the lamp at the end of the pier. It came on every evening at the same minute, whether the ferry was running or not, and it went off at dawn without anyone walking out to it. The harbourmaster said it was on a timer. The harbourmaster had also never been able to show anyone the timer.

I had been there three weeks when I decided to find it. I told myself it was curiosity, which was partly true. Mostly it was that I had started counting the minutes between the lamp coming on and the first boat leaving, and the number was always the same, and I did not like that it was always the same.

“You don’t want to go out there after dark,” the woman at the bakery said, handing me my change. She said it the way you would tell someone the milk had gone off. *You don’t want to.* Not *you mustn’t*.

***

The pier was longer at night. I know how that sounds. I counted the boards on the way out in the afternoon, two hundred and eleven of them, and I counted them again at nine o’clock with the lamp burning ahead of me, and I had passed three hundred before I stopped counting.

The lamp did not get closer. The water under the boards got louder.

When I turned around, the shore was exactly as far behind me as it should have been, which was the worst part, and the lamp was exactly as far ahead.

***

I left the island on the first ferry the next morning. The harbourmaster carried my bag down to the boat for me, which he had never done, and when I thanked him he looked past me down the pier.

“Two hundred and eleven,” he said. “It’s always two hundred and eleven in the daytime.”
